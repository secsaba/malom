# The board takes its size from the screen, and the phone page scrolls

This supersedes ADR-0007, which decided that the page is exactly as tall as the viewport, that the panel folds down to a handle on a phone, and that opening it takes its share of the height out of the board and gives it back on the way out.

On a screen at least 60rem wide nothing here changes: the panel stands beside the board and is always open, the board stops at 32rem so the room left over goes to the panel, and the page is one screen. Below that there is still only one column — a second would leave the board narrower than a phone gives it, which is the wrong way round — but the one column is no longer a screen with two things fitted into it. The board's drawn size comes from the screen, clamped between its floor and the width the screen gives it, and nothing the panel does enters into it. The panel stands below the board and is reached by scrolling. The phone page is as tall as what is on it.

What the board is and how it is drawn is unchanged. It draws itself to fit whatever box it is given, so one rule serves every screen; its ground is a shape inside the drawing rather than the element's background (`GROUND_CORNER` in `src/ui/board-layout.ts`), which is what lets the box be the wrong shape without the board looking it, and the drawing centres itself in whatever room it has. In one column it is uncapped and reaches both edges of the screen — the gutter comes out of the board rather than out of the finger, which is what keeps a point a fingertip wide on the narrowest phone still sold.

The decision is reopened by a fault ADR-0007's own cost section did not count. Redrawing the board at a different size on every toggle of the handle leaves the previous frame painted behind the current one when the site is run from the home screen as a web app. Measured against a reported screenshot at 393x852: the frame left behind is 320 wide at x=37, page-y 52, which is the board with the panel open; the board in front of it is 393 at x=0, which is the board with the panel shut. The two frames are both on screen at once. It does not happen in Safari itself, only in the home-screen web app, and it survives with an empty board, so it is not the piece shadow.

There is no seam at which the stale paint itself can be asserted — it reproduces in neither Playwright's WebKit nor Chromium, headless or headed. What can be asserted is the thing that causes it: that the board is redrawn at a different size at all. With the size constant across the toggle there is no resized drawing for the web view to keep a stale raster of, which is why the answer is to take the resize away rather than to chase the paint. ADR-0007 counted the cost of the fold as a board redrawn smaller while the panel is open. It did not count a board redrawn at all, on the one runtime a player who keeps the site to hand is most likely to be in.

The board's floor keeps its job and changes what it is read against. Height taken off the board is width taken off every one of its 24 targets — the board is 7.2 of its own steps across, so a target is the board over 7.2 — and a target below the 44px a fingertip needs is a board the player cannot play. The floor is 20rem, which is the board on the narrowest screen the site supports and the last one whose targets clear that. It is now one end of a clamp read against the screen: the board is drawn between the floor and the width the screen gives it, rather than into whatever height everything that cannot shrink left over. Where the board at the width of the screen is taller than the screen — the phone turned on its side is that case — the page scrolls, as it already did. A board to scroll to is worse than a board on one screen and far better than a board too small to hit, and that is as true when the scroll is the page's ordinary state as it was when it was the last resort.

Nothing the game is played with is scrolled away. Measured at 393x787, which is the reported phone run from the home screen: with teaching off the page comes to 918px, and the board — ending at y=524 — the status region at y=640, the teaching toggle, the difficulty and the setup are all above the fold, leaving the coordinates toggle and the language below it. With teaching on and five moves played the page comes to 1188px, and what stands above the fold is the board, the status region, the grade with its reason and the move list, with the difficulty, the setup, the coordinates toggle and the language below. Either way, a player scrolls to change how the game is set up, and never to play a move or to read what the last one was graded.

## Considered options

**Keeping the fold and forcing the web view to repaint** was an answer to the fault rather than to the decision: a repaint hint, a transform, a key that throws the drawing away and makes a new one. It was rejected because the fault reproduces in no browser that can be driven, so there would be nothing to hold such a fix to — a workaround with no test is a line nobody may ever safely delete — and it would leave every other consequence of resizing the board mid-game in place. Taking the resize away removes the class of fault rather than the one instance of it that was reported.

**Freezing the board's size and keeping the handle** is the fix and the whole of it: a board that holds still across a toggle that still happens is exactly what can be verified, so it is worth having on the way. It is not the end state, because a handle that costs the board nothing has nothing left to buy — it hides half the page in exchange for room the board no longer wants.

**Moving what the game needs out of the panel and into the play column** was the guard against the new scroll: the grade and the move list beside the board, so that nothing read mid-game is below the fold. The measurements say there is nothing to guard against, and the move would be paid for out of the play column's height, which is the board's width and the one budget with nothing spare in it.

**Opening the panel over the board as a bottom sheet**, which ADR-0007 also considered, is rejected again and for its own reason as well as that one: it keeps the board at full size behind a sheet covering the part of it the player cannot see either way, and it needs a scrim, a scroll lock and a way out of both. Standing the panel below the board needs none of them.

## Consequences

`tests/e2e/responsive.spec.ts` holds the acceptance and changes with the decision, measured on the app's own box as well as the document's. What it keeps: the board fills the width of a phone and stays whole on it; every point's target comes out at least 44px across at six widths from 320px up; a move is played by tapping twice on a touchscreen; nothing is drawn on top of anything else, with teaching off and on; and the panel stands beside the board with no handle at all once there is room for two columns. What it gains is the assertion the fault turns on: the board's drawn box is the same whatever the panel is doing. What it loses is the clause that said the phone page does not scroll down — that is now what the page is for. The direction that must still come back nought is sideways, at every width and on the phone turned on its side. The scroll down is asserted rather than merely permitted, and at the two shortest screens the site meets standing up — 320x568 and 360x640 — as well as at the phone and the phone on its side. They are where ADR-0007 claimed a page that was one screen and never was, and they are where the panel has the least room to stand below the board: what is asked of them is that scrolling to it is the whole of what the page does about that, with nothing drawn over anything else to spare the player the trip.

Later interface work maintains this. A block added to the panel no longer costs the board width, which is the budget ADR-0007 spent most of its argument protecting; it costs a scroll instead, and the panel's order is what the player pays it in — what is read during a move stands above what is only chosen between games. Anything that grows without bound still scrolls inside itself rather than running the page on without end. The three things that are never in the panel are the same three and for the same reasons: the board, whose turn it is, and the question a Blunder warning is put back with — a question nobody can see is a game that reads as stuck, and that argument never depended on the fold.

The panel has no open state left to hold. It folded because folding was how the board got its room back, and with nothing to give back there is nothing for a handle to control: the handle, the chevron beside it and the string written on it go with the decision. Nothing in the interface names the panel any more, which is what the **Panel** entry in `CONTEXT.md` now records — on a phone it is the blocks below the board, each shown under its own name.
